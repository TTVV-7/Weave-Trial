      Everything on the board runs on 5 V DC. The only mains part is the plug-in adapter, so use a sealed, certified 5 V adapter rather than an open-frame supply. Put a 10 A fuse in the 5 V line where it enters the cover. Each run gets its own pair of 5 V and GND wires from a thick bus inside the cover.       How it goes together

      A board, channels, and the strip

      Section from the wall to the front, at 5× life size, through the channel and across it. The strip stands on the board-side wall here so it shows; it can sit against either wall. Spacing between parts is schematic.
      • The board is any flat 600 × 600 mm sheet: 6 mm MDF or birch ply painted matte black, or black acrylic or Dibond if a sign shop cuts it.
      • The strip stands on its edge. A flat strip won't bend sideways while it lies flat, but on its edge it follows any smooth curve. It sticks to one wall with its own tape, LEDs facing across the channel.
      • The light bounces off the white wall opposite and comes out through the translucent cap. The cap covers the whole channel, so each line glows as one band about 15 mm wide, like neon.
      • The channels follow every line of the drawing, split into bed-sized pieces that butt together. Each one screws down through small tabs placed where they don't foul a neighbouring line.
      • Stars, eyes and mittens are shaped boxes: one LED cut from the strip lies in the bottom, under a lid of the same shape. The moon is a crescent box with strip running round the inside of its outer wall.
      • Every run ends over a hole, so the wires drop straight through and all the soldering happens on the back.
      • The standoffs hold the board clear of the wall, with room behind for the cover and the wiring. Each one hangs on a screw head by a keyhole.

      Build order

      Putting it together

      1. Test the strip first. Before cutting anything, run the firmware with the whole reel on the bench and check every LED lights.
      2. Print a test. Print brick_1 and its cap first. Stand a piece of strip in it and press the cap on. The cap should click in snugly, and the strip should glow evenly through it.
      3. Print the template (template_a4.pdf, actual size) and check the 100 mm scale bar. Tape the pages together using the 10 mm overlaps, then tape the template to the board.
      4. Drill through the template: 8 mm at every red wire hole, and a small pilot at each tab crosshair. Then paint the board.
      5. Screw the channels and shapes down on the pilots. The moon, stars and dots screw through their floors, with countersunk 3 mm screws. Their names are in the 3MF files and on the template.
      6. Cut the strip to the LED counts in the LED map, on the copper pads between LEDs. Solder three wires to each end, keeping track of the arrows: data goes in at the end the map says.
      7. Stand each run in its channel against one wall, with its own tape, and feed its wires through the hole. On a curve, put it against the outside wall of the bend. In the stars and dots, lay a single LED flat in the middle, facing up.
      8. Press the caps on. Do this last, after everything has lit up on test, since they're snug.
      9. Wire the back in LED-map order: each run's data out goes to the next run's data in. Bring 5 V and GND to every run from a pair of thick bus wires, rather than through the strip.
      10. Mount the controller under the cover: ESP32, a 74AHCT125 to lift the data line to 5 V, a 330 Ω resistor in the data line, and 1000 µF across the supply.
      11. Screw on the standoffs, flash the sketch, and hang it.
