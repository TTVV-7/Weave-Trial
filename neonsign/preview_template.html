      How it goes together

      A board, channels, and the flex

      Section from the wall to the front, drawn about 5× life size. Spacing between parts is schematic.
      • The board is any flat 600 × 600 mm sheet: 6 mm MDF or birch ply painted matte black, or black acrylic or Dibond if a sign shop cuts it.
      • The channels follow every line of the drawing, split into bed-sized pieces that butt together. Each one screws down through small tabs placed where they don't foul a neighbouring line.
      • The flex presses into the channel with about 4 mm standing proud, so it reads as a glowing tube from the side as well as head-on.
      • Every run ends over a hole, so the wires drop straight through and all the soldering happens on the back.
      • Stars, eyes and mittens are single 12 mm pixels pushed through the board from behind, under printed translucent caps.
      • The standoffs hold the board clear of the wall, with room behind for the cover and the wiring. Each one hangs on a screw head by a keyhole.

      Build order

      Putting it together

      1. Test the flex first. Before cutting anything, run the firmware with the whole reel on the bench and check every LED lights.
      2. Print the template (template_a4.pdf, actual size) and check the 100 mm scale bar. Tape the pages together using the 10 mm overlaps, then tape the template to the board.
      3. Drill through the template: 8 mm at every red wire hole, 12 mm for each pixel, and a small pilot at each tab crosshair. Then paint the board.
      4. Screw the channels down on the pilots. Their names are in the 3MF files and on the template.
      5. Cut the flex to the lengths in the LED map, at the cut marks. Fit end caps with the wires coming out of the bottom, and seal them with the kit's silicone.
      6. Press each run into its channel, feeding its wires through the hole. A few dots of clear silicone hold it if it's loose.
      7. Wire the back in LED-map order: each run's data out goes to the next run's data in. Bring 5 V and GND to every run from a pair of thick bus wires, rather than through the flex.
      8. Mount the controller under the cover: ESP32, a 74AHCT125 to lift the data line to 5 V, a 330 Ω resistor in the data line, and 1000 µF across the supply.
      9. Screw on the standoffs, flash the sketch, and hang it.
