Santa down the chimney

A WS2812B LED strip standing on its edge in 3D-printed channels, stuck open side first to the inside of a frosted glass door, which diffuses the light. Santa waves, sinks until only his hat shows, vanishes down the chimney, the fire flares, and he climbs back out.

      Everything on the sign runs on 5 V DC. The only mains part is the plug-in adapter, so use a sealed, certified 5 V adapter rather than an open-frame supply. Put a 10 A fuse in the 5 V line where it enters the cover. Each run gets its own pair of 5 V and GND wires from a thick bus inside the cover. Those aren't drawn here, to keep the data route readable.

      Build order

      Putting it together

      1. Test the strip first. Before cutting anything, run the firmware with the whole reel on the bench and check every LED lights.
      2. Print a test. Print brick_1 first, stand a piece of strip in it, and hold it against the glass in the dark. Check you like the glow before printing the rest.
      3. Mark out the glass. Print template_a4.pdf at actual size and check the 100 mm scale bar. It's drawn as seen from inside. Tape the pages together and tape the sheet to the outside of the door, where you can see it through the glass, or mark each part's corners on the inside with masking tape.
      4. Cut the strip to the LED counts in the LED map, on the copper pads between LEDs. Solder three wires to each end, keeping track of the arrows: data goes in at the end the map says.
      5. Fit the strip into each part before it goes on the glass. Stand each run against one wall with its own tape, and feed its wires out through the slot in the floor. On a curve, put it against the outside wall of the bend. In the stars and dots, lay a single LED flat in the middle, facing the open side.
      6. Clean the glass with isopropyl alcohol where the parts go.
      7. Stick each part on, open side first, with clear double-sided mounting tape on the flange. Press it firmly for 30 seconds. Start from the middle of the drawing and work outwards.
      8. Wire it up in LED-map order: each run's data out goes to the next run's data in. Bring 5 V and GND to every run from a pair of thick bus wires, rather than through the strip. Clip the wires to the door so they don't pull on the parts.
      9. Mount the controller in its cover on the inside of the door or the frame: ESP32, a 74AHCT125 to lift the data line to 5 V, a 330 Ω resistor in the data line, and 1000 µF across the supply. Then flash the sketch.

      2. Print a test. Print brick_1 and its cap first. Stand a piece of strip in it and press the cap on. The cap should click in snugly, and the strip should glow evenly through it.

      4. Drill through the template: 8 mm at every red wire hole, and a small pilot at each tab crosshair. Then paint the board.
      5. Screw the channels and shapes down on the pilots. The moon, stars and dots screw through their floors, with countersunk 3 mm screws. Their names are in the 3MF files and on the template.
      6. Cut the strip to the LED counts in the LED map, on the copper pads between LEDs. Solder three wires to each end, keeping track of the arrows: data goes in at the end the map says.
      7. Stand each run in its channel against one wall, with its own tape, and feed its wires through the hole. On a curve, put it against the outside wall of the bend. In the stars and dots, lay a single LED flat in the middle, facing up.
      8. Press the caps on. Do this last, after everything has lit up on test, since they're snug.
      9. Wire the back in LED-map order: each run's data out goes to the next run's data in. Bring 5 V and GND to every run from a pair of thick bus wires, rather than through the strip.
      10. Mount the controller under the cover: ESP32, a 74AHCT125 to lift the data line to 5 V, a 330 Ω resistor in the data line, and 1000 µF across the supply.
      11. Screw on the standoffs, flash the sketch, and hang it.
